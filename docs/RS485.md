# USART1 / RS-485 Bring-up

Status: the active, receive-first USART1 transport and read-only native v1
protocol are implemented and bench-proven. Complete commands and responses
work through the tested board's physical `485_A2`/`485_B2` connector. Exact
reset-time and turnaround waveforms remain to be scoped.

## Hardware contract

The published MKS SERVO57D RS-485 V1.1 schematic routes:

| MCU signal | Board net | Transceiver connection |
| --- | --- | --- |
| PC13 GPIO | `RE1` | Tied SP485E `/RE` and `DE` controls on the tested board |
| PA9 USART1 AF4 | `TX` / `TXD` | SP485E `DI` through 470 ohms |
| PA10 USART1 AF4 | `RX` / `RXD` | SP485E `RO` through 470 ohms |

The SP485E receiver enable is active-low and its driver enable is active-high.
Because the two pins are tied together, PC13 low selects receive and PC13 high
selects transmit. Firmware preloads PC13 low before changing it to an output,
and every transfer-error path drives it low.

The published schematic labels create an important conflict: PA8 is `nDIR`,
while user tracing and successful operation identify PC13 `RE1` as the
populated RS-485 direction path. The physical `485_A2`/`485_B2` connector is
also the working port even though the apparent schematic routing suggests the
other connector. Firmware cannot guarantee the interval before PC13 takes
control, so reset-time direction and bus state remain oscilloscope measurements
and must be rechecked on other board revisions.

The N32L40x pin tables identify PA9/PA10 as USART1 TX/RX on AF4. The firmware
uses asynchronous 115200 baud, 8 data bits, no parity, and one stop bit at the
current 4 MHz peripheral clock. Its rounded divider produces approximately
114286 baud, about 0.8 percent below nominal.

## DMA ownership

The transport implements the channel allocation in the real-time architecture:

- DMA channel 4 continuously moves USART1 receive bytes into a 256-byte
  circular buffer at medium DMA priority.
- Half-transfer and transfer-complete events wake foreground work at NVIC
  priority 8; they do not parse bytes.
- USART IDLE and error events also run at priority 8. IDLE is only a transfer
  boundary hint; native v1 uses an explicit delimiter, while a future Modbus
  adapter will own its required silence and timeout policy.
- DMA channel 5 moves one foreground-validated transmit frame, up to 256 bytes,
  from a fixed staging buffer into USART1.
- DMA completion disables the TX DMA request but does not release PC13. The
  USART transmission-complete interrupt releases PC13 only after the final stop
  bit has left the shifter.

The main loop drains at most 64 received bytes per pass into the native v1
streaming parser. Foreground owns COBS decoding, length and CRC validation,
address filtering, command dispatch, response encoding, and the bounded
`rs485_write()` call. The first slice responds only to valid address-1 ping,
identity, and capability requests. It never transmits at boot or in response
to noise, malformed frames, broadcasts, foreign addresses, responses, or
events. See [command protocol](PROTOCOL.md).

The retained bridge characterizer suspends foreground RX draining and protocol parsing while the
bridge characterizer displays `RUN`. Circular DMA reception continues, but no
RS-485 command starts, stops, selects, or otherwise alters the bridge waveform.

If foreground falls a complete buffer behind, the cursor discards the oldest
overwritten bytes and reports both an overrun event and the exact dropped-byte
count. A DMA transfer error stops the transport without an ISR retry, disables
both DMA requests, and restores receive direction. Recovery requires explicit
reinitialization.

## Diagnostic evidence

Diagnostic schema 4 preserves the schema-3 transport prefix and publishes
transport status, total received bytes, USART
IDLE/error counts, ring overruns and dropped bytes, the last drained byte, and
TX byte/frame/error/busy state. It appends native-parser readiness, accepted
frames, responses, framing/CRC/version errors, filtered traffic, and TX
rejections. The RAM record remains a separate observability channel; firmware
does not serialize its C layout or transmit unsolicited status.

## Bench result and remaining validation

At 115200 8N1, valid native commands receive complete responses through
`485_A2`/`485_B2`, establishing the USART mapping, PC13 direction polarity,
transceiver path, connector identity, framing, CRC, dispatch, and ordinary
turnaround behavior.

Remaining electrical work is to scope PC13, TXD, A, and B through power-on,
reset, and final-stop-bit turnaround; verify the actual baud error; exercise
buffer limits and injected line faults; and confirm bridge commands return to
the documented all-low vector through communication errors.
